How to Draw a Watermelon Slice

An easy step-by-step drawing for kids and beginners: 11 steps, about 3 minutes, on paper.

  • 11steps
  • 3 minabout

Quick answer

Draw a watermelon slice in 11 steps: draw the left side, draw the right side, draw the bottom, then carry on one line at a time and color it in. It takes about 3 minutes with a pencil or a pen.

  • Steps: 11
  • Time: about 3 minutes
  • You need: paper, a pencil or a pen, something to color with
  • From: the Fruits path in Paper Coach

This watermelon slice starts as a tall triangle with a bowl-shaped bottom. Two curves near the bottom turn it into a rind with a thin stripe, and five small teardrops become the seeds. It's the first lesson on the Fruits path and a calm, quick drawing to share with a child.

Tips

Draw a watermelon slice well

Tips for drawing a watermelon slice

  • Make the triangle taller than it is wide. A slice about twice as tall as its bottom edge looks like a real wedge.
  • Let the bottom curve dip well below the two corners. A deeper bowl makes the slice look round and juicy.
  • Draw the rind's top line and the stripe so they follow the same bowl shape as the bottom. Curves that stay evenly spaced look neat.
  • Keep the seeds small, about the size of a fingertip on the page, so they don't crowd the pink inside.
  • Color the big pink area first, then the green bands, and the black seeds last so they stay crisp.

Common mistakes

  • The bottom is drawn as a straight line, so the slice looks like a flat triangle. Join the corners with a curve that sags low in the middle, like the bottom of a bowl.
  • The stripe is squeezed so tight it disappears once colored. Leave a small gap between the rind's top line and the bottom, then place the stripe curve halfway between them.
  • Seeds point in all directions or bunch up in one corner. Point every teardrop up. Put two near the top and three in an even row below them.

Make it your own

  • Draw a wider, shorter triangle for a fat slice, keeping the same rind, stripe and five seeds.
  • Draw two slices side by side, one a little taller, to make a pair to color together.
  • Try a lighter or deeper pink for the inside to see how it changes the look of the slice.
  • Tilt the whole triangle a little to one side so the slice looks like it's leaning.

Questions

Good to know

How do I make the two sides of the slice even?

Start both sides from the same top point and stop them at the same height. Glance at the gap between the corners and the point to check they match.

Why does the bottom of a watermelon slice curve?

A watermelon is round, so a slice cut from it has a curved outer edge. The bowl-shaped bottom shows that roundness and makes the rind follow it.

How many seeds should a watermelon slice have?

This drawing uses five: two near the top and three in a row underneath. Five fills the pink area nicely without making it busy.

How can a young child draw the seeds more easily?

Let them draw small dots first, then pull a short point up from each dot. That turns each dot into a teardrop.

How long does it take to draw a watermelon slice?

About 3 minutes for the lines, and a few more to color it in.

Can kids follow this guide?

Yes, children from about six can follow it on their own, and younger ones with a grown-up beside them.

What do I need?

Paper and a pencil or a pen, and crayons, markers or colored pencils for the colors. An eraser helps but isn’t needed.
