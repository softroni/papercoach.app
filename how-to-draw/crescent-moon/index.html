How to Draw a Crescent Moon

An easy step-by-step drawing for kids and beginners: 7 steps, about 2 minutes, on paper.

  • 7steps
  • 2 minabout
  • Colorat the end

Quick answer

Draw a crescent moon in 7 steps: draw the outer curve, draw the inner curve, draw the top crater, then carry on one line at a time and color it in. It takes about 2 minutes with a pencil or a pen.

  • Steps: 7
  • Time: about 2 minutes
  • You need: paper, a pencil or a pen, something to color with
  • From: the Space path in Paper Coach

This crescent moon starts as a fat letter C. A second, gentler curve joins the same two tips and takes a bite out of it. Three circles in three sizes turn the shape into a moon with craters. It is quick and friendly, and a good warm-up before the planets and spacecraft later on this path.

Tips

Draw a crescent moon well

Tips for drawing a crescent moon

  • Make the outer curve round and tall, almost like half a circle, so the thick middle has room for all three craters.
  • Start the inner curve right on the bottom tip and finish right on the top tip, so both points come out sharp.
  • Bend the inner curve less than the outer one. The gap between them should be widest in the middle and shrink to nothing at the tips.
  • Stack the craters down the thick side: a small one near the top, the biggest at the widest spot, the smallest underneath.
  • Color the yellow first and the craters last, keeping the gray inside each circle line.

Common mistakes

  • The inner curve bulges as much as the outer one, so the moon turns into a thin sliver. Draw the inner curve flatter. The thickest part of the moon should be about a third as wide as the moon is tall.
  • The craters touch or cross the moon's edges. Leave a small gap between each circle and both curves, so every crater sits fully inside the yellow.
  • All three craters end up the same size. Make the middle crater clearly the biggest, the top one medium, and the bottom one the smallest.

Make it your own

  • Mirror the shape into a backward C so the moon faces the other way.
  • Try a pale cream or white moon instead of yellow, with darker gray craters.
  • Draw two crescents, one big and one small, facing each other across the page.
  • Add a few tiny extra circles in the thick side for a bumpier, older-looking moon.

Questions

Good to know

How do I keep the tips of the crescent pointy?

Bring the inner curve all the way to each tip of the outer curve and stop exactly there. If the lines miss each other or round off, the points go blunt.

Which way should a crescent moon face?

Either way works. Drawn as a C, the bite opens to the right. Flip it into a backward C if you want the bite on the left.

Why does my moon look like a banana?

Both curves are probably long and thin. Make the outer curve rounder and the inner curve gentler, so the middle gets fat and the moon looks bitten, not stretched.

Why put the biggest crater in the middle?

The middle is the widest part of the crescent, so it is the only spot with room for the large circle. Smaller craters fit higher and lower where the moon narrows.

How long does it take to draw a crescent moon?

About 2 minutes for the lines, and a few more to color it in.

Can kids follow this guide?

Yes, children from about six can follow it on their own, and younger ones with a grown-up beside them.

What do I need?

Paper and a pencil or a pen, and crayons, markers or colored pencils for the colors. An eraser helps but isn’t needed.
