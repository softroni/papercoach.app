How to Draw a Shield

An easy step-by-step drawing for kids and beginners: 9 steps, about 3 minutes, on paper.

  • 9steps
  • 3 minabout
  • Colorat the end

Quick answer

Draw a shield in 9 steps: draw the shield, draw the rim, draw the boss, then carry on one line at a time and color it in. It takes about 3 minutes with a pencil or a pen.

  • Steps: 9
  • Time: about 3 minutes
  • You need: paper, a pencil or a pen, something to color with
  • From: the Fantasy Objects path in Paper Coach

This shield looks like it came off a knight's arm in a fantasy castle. You start with one outline, then add a rim, a round boss in the center and two lines that split it into four quarters. Ten gold rivets and a pattern of blue and red finish it.

Tips

Draw a shield well

Tips for drawing a shield

  • Lightly mark the middle of the top edge first. Draw the left half down to the point, then copy it on the right so both sides match.
  • Keep the rim band the same width all the way around, about a finger's width on most paper, even where it narrows into the point.
  • Make the boss roughly a quarter as wide as the shield. Too small and it gets lost, too big and the quarters disappear.
  • Space the rivets evenly before you draw them. Small dots in pencil first help you fit three per side.
  • Color the matching corners together: top left with bottom right, then top right with bottom left.

Common mistakes

  • The two quarter lines run over the boss, so it looks flat and cut in pieces. Stop each line at the edge of the circle and pick it up again on the other side, so the boss sits on top.
  • The inner outline drifts closer to the edge in some places, making the rim lumpy. Follow the outer line slowly and keep the same small gap, checking the corners and the point as you go.
  • The rivets crowd together on one side and leave a gap on the other. Put the corner rivets down first, then place the middle one halfway between them on each side.

Make it your own

  • Swap the colors for your own pair, such as green and yellow, still matching across the corners.
  • Make one side of the quarters a single color and the other side two colors for a different crest.
  • Try a taller, narrower shield outline with the same rim, boss and ten rivets.
  • Color the rim a darker gray along one edge to suggest it catches the light differently.

Questions

Good to know

How do I get both sides of the shield even?

Draw from the middle of the top edge outward. Finish one half, then mirror it, matching where the curve starts to bend toward the point. Turning the paper upside down helps you spot a lopsided side.

Why do the quarter lines go behind the boss?

Real shields have the boss bolted on top of the painted face. Leaving the lines hidden behind the circle makes the boss look raised instead of drawn flat onto the colors.

Where does the tenth rivet go?

Three sit along the top, three down each side, and the last one goes at the bottom point of the rim. That single rivet ties the two sides together.

Which quarters should be blue and which red?

Colors match across the corners. If the top left is blue, the bottom right is blue too, and the other two quarters are red. Either color can start at the top left.

How long does it take to draw a shield?

About 3 minutes for the lines, and a few more to color it in.

Can kids follow this guide?

Yes, children from about six can follow it on their own, and younger ones with a grown-up beside them.

What do I need?

Paper and a pencil or a pen, and crayons, markers or colored pencils for the colors. An eraser helps but isn’t needed.
