How to Draw a Shop Front

An easy step-by-step drawing for kids and beginners: 16 steps, about 5 minutes, on paper.

  • 16steps
  • 5 minabout
  • Colorat the end

Quick answer

Draw a shop front in 16 steps: draw the wall, draw the lid, draw the sign, then carry on one line at a time and color it in. It takes about 5 minutes with a pencil or a pen.

  • Steps: 16
  • Time: about 5 minutes
  • You need: paper, a pencil or a pen, something to color with
  • From: the Around Town path in Paper Coach

This bookshop starts as one big box, and every other part fits inside or around it. You stack a lid and a sign on top, hang a bumpy striped awning, and fill the front with a window, two panels and a door. A potted bush and a tree finish the street scene before you color it.

Tips

Draw a shop front well

Tips for drawing a shop front

  • Make the wall box wide, about one and a half times as wide as it is tall, so the window, panels and door all have room side by side.
  • Before the stripes, find the six points where two bumps meet along the awning's bottom edge. Each stripe line runs from the top edge down to one of those points.
  • Keep the window on one side and the door on the other. The door's bottom edge sits right on the wall's bottom line, because it stands on the ground.
  • Line up the books' bottoms on the window's lower edge, then step their heights up toward the middle book and back down.
  • Draw the tree's bumpy outline only where it peeks out past the wall. Start and stop each end right on the wall's side or top line.

Common mistakes

  • The awning comes out as a plain box with straight sides. Keep the top line level and let both sides lean outward as they go down, so the bottom is wider than the top.
  • The stripes end in the middle of a bump, so they look crooked. End every stripe line exactly where two bumps meet. Seven bumps give you seven stripes from six lines.
  • The pot is drawn as a straight box and looks like a crate. Make the pot wider at the top and narrower at the bottom, then sit the bush's bumps on its top edge.

Make it your own

  • Write a shop name in the blank inner box of the sign, using short, simple capital letters.
  • Swap the red and cream awning for two other stripe colors, and color two books to match.
  • Turn it into a different shop by changing the five books into cakes, flowers or toys in the window.
  • Add a second potted bush on the right side of the door so the front looks balanced.

Questions

Good to know

How do I make the awning bumps even?

Mark the bottom edge into seven equal parts with tiny dots first. Then draw one rounded bump between each pair of dots, keeping them all about the same depth.

Why is the lid wider than the wall?

A lid that sticks out a little past both sides reads as a roof edge. If it matches the wall exactly, the top of the shop looks flat and unfinished.

How do I make the window look like glass?

Draw two short slanted lines near one corner of the inner box, running in the same direction. That small shine tells the eye it is glass, and the blue color helps too.

Can a child draw this bookshop?

Yes. Every part is a box, a line or a row of bumps. A parent can draw the big wall box and let the child add the books, bush and stripes.

How long does it take to draw a shop front?

About 5 minutes for the lines, and a few more to color it in.

Can kids follow this guide?

Yes, children from about six can follow it on their own, and younger ones with a grown-up beside them.

What do I need?

Paper and a pencil or a pen, and crayons, markers or colored pencils for the colors. An eraser helps but isn’t needed.
