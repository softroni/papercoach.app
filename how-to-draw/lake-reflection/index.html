How to Draw a Lake Reflection

An easy step-by-step drawing for kids and beginners: 12 steps, about 3 minutes, on paper.

  • 12steps
  • 3 minabout
  • Colorat the end

Quick answer

Draw a lake reflection in 12 steps: draw the shore, draw the lake, draw the mountain, then carry on one line at a time and color it in. It takes about 3 minutes with a pencil or a pen.

  • Steps: 12
  • Time: about 3 minutes
  • You need: paper, a pencil or a pen, something to color with
  • From: the Landscape path in Paper Coach

This lake scene starts with one straight shoreline. A pointed mountain and three narrow pines stand on top of it, and below the line the water shows each one again, flipped upside down. The reflections are colored paler than the real shapes, and that small change makes the water look like water.

Tips

Draw a lake reflection well

Tips for drawing a lake reflection

  • Draw the shoreline straight across the middle of the page. It is the mirror line, so a tilted or wavy shore makes every reflection come out crooked.
  • Before you draw each reflection, measure how tall the shape is above the line. Make the upside-down copy the same height below it.
  • Draw the mountain and its reflection a little wider than the three trees together, so the peak stays the biggest shape in the scene.
  • Keep the four ripples short and flat, each about as long as one tree is wide. Space them out so the reflections still show between them.
  • Color the reflections lighter than the shapes above. Gray under the purple mountain and pale green under dark green trees reads as water.

Common mistakes

  • The reflection points up, so it looks like a second mountain standing on the shore. Start the reflection at the shoreline and draw its point downward, into the lake, right below the real peak.
  • The lake curve is too shallow, and the reflections hang out past the bottom of the bowl. Draw the reflections first in light pencil, then make the bowl curve deep enough to hold them.
  • The reflections get the same strong colors as the mountain and trees, and the scene looks flat. Press lightly or pick a lighter shade for anything below the shoreline. The water copy should always look softer.

Make it your own

  • Put the three trees to the left of the mountain instead, and flip their reflections to match.
  • Make the trees different heights, tall in the middle and shorter on each side, and give every reflection the same height as its tree.
  • Draw two mountains side by side, one shorter than the other, and mirror both in the lake.
  • Add a few more ripple lines near the bottom of the bowl, keeping them short and flat.

Questions

Good to know

Why does the reflection go upside down?

Calm water works like a mirror lying flat. The base of the mountain touches the shoreline, so its reflection starts there too, and the peak ends up pointing down into the lake.

Why is the reflection a different color from the mountain?

Water shows a dimmer, softer copy of what stands above it. Coloring the reflection gray or pale tells the eye it is looking at water, not at a second solid shape.

What do the ripple lines do?

They show the surface of the water. A few short, flat lines across the reflections make the lake feel like it lies flat and still, without hiding the mirrored shapes.

How can I make the reflection match the real shape?

Copy the same width at the shoreline, then the same height. A light dot below the line, as far down as the peak is up, gives you a target to draw toward.

How long does it take to draw a lake reflection?

About 3 minutes for the lines, and a few more to color it in.

Can kids follow this guide?

Yes, children from about six can follow it on their own, and younger ones with a grown-up beside them.

What do I need?

Paper and a pencil or a pen, and crayons, markers or colored pencils for the colors. An eraser helps but isn’t needed.
