How to Draw a Submarine

An easy step-by-step drawing for kids and beginners: 8 steps, about 2 minutes, on paper.

  • 8steps
  • 2 minabout
  • Colorat the end

Quick answer

Draw a submarine in 8 steps: draw the top half, draw the bottom half, draw the tower, then carry on one line at a time and color it in. It takes about 2 minutes with a pencil or a pen.

  • Steps: 8
  • Time: about 2 minutes
  • You need: paper, a pencil or a pen, something to color with
  • From: the On the Water path in Paper Coach

This submarine starts as two matching arches, one on top and one underneath, that join into a long capsule. A rounded tower, a periscope shaped like an upside-down L, and three round portholes finish the shape. Then three colors fill it in: yellow, orange and blue.

Tips

Draw a submarine well

Tips for drawing a submarine

  • Make the body a little more than twice as long as it is tall. A long capsule reads as a submarine. A short one looks more like an egg.
  • Start the bottom arch at the same left point where the top arch began, so the two halves meet cleanly at both ends.
  • Keep the tower narrow, about a quarter of the body's length, and center it on the top line.
  • Leave a little space between the portholes and the body's edge, so each circle sits fully inside the capsule.
  • Color the large yellow body first with long strokes, then switch to small careful strokes for the orange tower and blue portholes.

Common mistakes

  • The bottom half is much flatter or deeper than the top, so the body looks lopsided. Glance at the top arch while drawing the bottom one and match its height, like a mirror image.
  • The periscope points backward or straight up with no bend. Draw a short line straight up from the tower, then turn it toward the front end so it looks ahead.
  • The three portholes come out different sizes or drift up and down. Draw the middle circle first, then copy its size on each side, keeping all three centers on one level line.

Make it your own

  • Swap the colors: try a red body with a yellow tower, keeping the portholes blue so they still look like glass.
  • Make the capsule longer and add more portholes in the same row for a bigger submarine.
  • Draw two or three submarines in a line, each a little smaller, like a family traveling together.
  • Turn the periscope the other way and draw the submarine facing left instead of right.

Questions

Good to know

How do I keep the submarine body from looking like a plain oval?

Stretch it out. Keep the top and bottom lines fairly flat through the middle and round only the two ends. That long, even capsule shape is what makes it a submarine.

Where should the tower go on a submarine?

Place it on the top line, near the middle of the body. It should be much smaller than the body, with straight sides and a rounded top.

Why does the periscope bend at the top?

The bend lets the periscope look ahead toward the front of the submarine. Drawing it as an upside-down letter L makes that turn easy to get right.

Is this a good submarine drawing for young kids?

Yes. It uses only arches, a rounded tower, one bent line and three circles. A parent can draw the body while a child adds the portholes and colors.

How long does it take to draw a submarine?

About 2 minutes for the lines, and a few more to color it in.

Can kids follow this guide?

Yes, children from about six can follow it on their own, and younger ones with a grown-up beside them.

What do I need?

Paper and a pencil or a pen, and crayons, markers or colored pencils for the colors. An eraser helps but isn’t needed.
