How to Draw a Rocket

An easy step-by-step drawing for kids and beginners: 11 steps, about 3 minutes, on paper.

Quick answer

Draw a rocket in 11 steps: draw the left side, draw the right side, draw the nose line, then carry on one line at a time and color it in. It takes about 3 minutes with a pencil or a pen.

  • Steps: 11
  • Time: about 3 minutes
  • You need: paper, a pencil or a pen, something to color with
  • From: the Space path in Paper Coach

This rocket is a tall, pointed body built from two matching curves, with a line across the top for the nose cone. Add a round window, two fins and a flame with a smaller flame inside it. It's the first drawing on the Space path, and it's simple enough for a young child to finish with a parent.

Step by step

Each picture shows the lines so far: the faded ones are done, the dark ones are this step’s.

  1. Step 1: Draw the left side

    Start at the tip of the nose. Draw the left side, curving down to the bottom.

  2. Step 2: Draw the right side

    Back to the tip. Take the right side down the same way, and close the bottom.

  3. Step 3: Draw the nose line

    One straight line across, near the top. Everything above it is the nose cone.

Tips for drawing a rocket

  • Make the body about three times as tall as it is wide. That gives the rocket a slim, fast look.
  • Before you draw the right side, look back at the left one. Try to mirror its curve so both sides bulge out the same amount.
  • Put the nose line about a quarter of the way down from the tip. That way the cone looks like its own part.
  • Draw the window a little above the middle of the body. That leaves clear space between it and the nose line, and between it and the fins.
  • Make the flame about half as tall as the body. Then draw the inner flame so it fills about half of the outer one.

Common mistakes

  • The two sides don't match, so the rocket leans or one side looks fatter. Start both sides at the same tip and stop them at the same height. Then join the bottom with one flat line.
  • The window is so big that it touches the sides of the body. Keep the circle to about half the body's width, so there is space around it on all sides.
  • The fins stick straight out sideways, like arms. Angle each fin down and back toward the bottom. Let its lowest point sit at the base of the rocket or a little below it.

Make it your own

  • Draw two or three windows stacked down the middle of the body instead of one.
  • Swap the colors. Try a white body with a blue nose cone and blue fins.
  • Make the flame longer and thinner so the rocket looks like it's speeding up.
  • Tilt the whole rocket on the page so it looks like it's flying off at an angle.

Questions

How do I get a sharp point at the top of the rocket?

Start both side lines from the exact same dot. Make each curve lean in gently right at the top, so the two lines meet in a point and don't flatten out.

Why does the flame have a smaller flame inside it?

The inner teardrop makes the flame look hot and bright. Orange around the outside and yellow in the center look like real fire.

Can a young child draw this rocket?

Yes. It's mostly two curved lines, a straight line, a circle and two teardrops. A parent can draw the first side, and the child can copy it for the second.

What should I draw around my rocket?

Keep it simple. A few stars or a round moon in the empty space works well. Later lessons on this path cover moons and planets.

How long does it take to draw a rocket?

About 3 minutes for the lines, and a few more to color it in.

Can kids follow this guide?

Yes, children from about six can follow it on their own, and younger ones with a grown-up beside them.

What do I need?

Paper and a pencil or a pen, and crayons, markers or colored pencils for the colors. An eraser helps but isn’t needed.
