How to Draw a Rain Cloud

An easy step-by-step drawing for kids and beginners: 7 steps, about 2 minutes, on paper.

  • 7steps
  • 2 minabout
  • Colorat the end

Quick answer

Draw a rain cloud in 7 steps: draw the base, draw the left bumps, draw the right bumps, then carry on one line at a time and color it in. It takes about 2 minutes with a pencil or a pen.

  • Steps: 7
  • Time: about 2 minutes
  • You need: paper, a pencil or a pen, something to color with
  • From: the Sky & Weather path in Paper Coach

This rain cloud starts with one flat line, adds four rounded bumps above it, and then drops five slanted teardrops below. It's a good next step after a plain cloud, because it adds a second shape that repeats in a neat pattern. A child can follow along easily, and the drops make good practice for drawing the same shape more than once.

Tips

Draw a rain cloud well

Tips for drawing a rain cloud

  • Make the base line about as long as you want the whole cloud. Each bump starts and ends on that line, so the line sets the cloud's width.
  • Let the tall left bump be the highest point of the cloud, about twice the height of the small bump next to it.
  • Slant every drop the same way, like rain blown by one breeze. Matching angles make the five drops look like they belong together.
  • Leave a gap of about one drop's width between the base and the top row, so the rain looks like it is falling.
  • Place each drop in the bottom row right under a gap in the top row, so the five drops form a zigzag.

Common mistakes

  • The last right bump ends above the base line, so the cloud is left open at one end. Bring the final puff all the way down until it touches the right end of the flat line and closes the shape.
  • Drops come out round like circles or pointed at the bottom. Start each drop with a sharp point on top, then curve out into a round bottom, like an upside-down ice cream cone.
  • The two bottom drops end up directly under top drops and get crowded. Drop them lower and slide each one sideways so it sits beneath the space between two top drops.

Make it your own

  • Add a third row of drops, or more, under the gaps of the bottom row for a heavier shower.
  • Draw two rain clouds side by side, one a bit smaller and lower, so they overlap at the edges.
  • Use a darker gray on the cloud for a stormy day, and keep the drops a bright blue so they stand out.
  • Make the drops smaller and closer together for a light drizzle instead of big, heavy rain.

Questions

Good to know

Why does the cloud start with a flat line instead of the bumps?

The flat line gives the bumps a floor to start and end on. It keeps the cloud from tilting and makes a clean bottom edge, so the raindrops have a clear place to fall from.

How do I draw a raindrop shape?

Start at a point, curve one side out and around into a round bottom, then curve back up to the same point. Tilt it a little so the point leans to one side.

Should the cloud's bumps all be the same size?

No. Mixing sizes looks more natural. Here the left side goes small then tall, and the two right puffs step back down to the base line.

How can my child keep the raindrops evenly spaced?

Draw the middle drop of the top row first, then one on each side at equal distances. The two lower drops then go under the two gaps.

How long does it take to draw a rain cloud?

About 2 minutes for the lines, and a few more to color it in.

Can kids follow this guide?

Yes, children from about six can follow it on their own, and younger ones with a grown-up beside them.

What do I need?

Paper and a pencil or a pen, and crayons, markers or colored pencils for the colors. An eraser helps but isn’t needed.
