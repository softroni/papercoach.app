How to Draw Sea Cliffs

An easy step-by-step drawing for kids and beginners: 12 steps, about 3 minutes, on paper.

  • 12steps
  • 3 minabout
  • Colorat the end

Quick answer

Draw sea cliffs in 12 steps: draw the left edge and top, draw the cliff face, draw the far cliff, then carry on one line at a time and color it in. It takes about 3 minutes with a pencil or a pen.

  • Steps: 12
  • Time: about 3 minutes
  • You need: paper, a pencil or a pen, something to color with
  • From: the Landscape path in Paper Coach

This scene shows depth without a single building. The near cliff stands tall and dark, and a lower, paler cliff sits behind it, so your eye reads the distance at once. A lone rock in the water and three waves that shrink toward the horizon finish the sense of space.

Tips

Draw sea cliffs well

Tips for drawing sea cliffs

  • Make the near cliff much taller than the far cliff, close to three times its height. The big difference makes the far one feel farther away.
  • Start the far cliff where it disappears behind the near cliff's face, so the near cliff overlaps it and sits in front.
  • Use a ruler or a slow steady hand for the horizon. Keep both pieces at exactly the same height, as if the line runs behind the cliff and rock.
  • Space the three waves about evenly down the sea, and make each one longer than the wave above it.
  • Keep the grass line close under each top edge, like a thin cap, not a thick band halfway down the cliff.

Common mistakes

  • Drawing the far cliff the same height as the near one. Bring its top down well below the near cliff's top and push it to the right, so it clearly steps back.
  • Letting the two horizon pieces sit at different heights. Line them up before drawing the second piece. The sea is flat, so the line behind the rock must match.
  • Making all three waves the same length. Draw the lowest wave longest and each wave above it shorter. Shorter waves read as farther out at sea.

Make it your own

  • Add a second, smaller sea stack farther out, with its base right on the horizon line, so it looks distant.
  • Draw a third cliff behind the far one, even lower and paler, to push the coastline deeper into the scene.
  • Flip the scene so the tall cliff stands on the right and the far cliff steps down to the left.
  • Try a sunset sky by coloring above the horizon in soft orange while keeping the sea blue.

Questions

Good to know

How do I make the back cliff look farther away?

Make it lower than the near cliff, tuck its edge behind the near cliff, and color it a pale cream instead of brown. Smaller, overlapped and lighter all say distance.

Why should the sea stack's top rise above the horizon?

A rock that pokes above the line feels close and tall. If it stayed below the horizon, it would look tiny and far out at sea.

Why is the horizon drawn in two pieces?

The sea stack stands in front of the horizon and hides part of it. Stopping the line at the rock and starting again on the other side keeps the rock in front.

How wavy should the wave lines be?

Use gentle, low bumps, a few per line. Big tall loops look like hills. Small ripples keep the sea looking flat and calm.

How long does it take to draw sea cliffs?

About 3 minutes for the lines, and a few more to color it in.

Can kids follow this guide?

Yes, children from about six can follow it on their own, and younger ones with a grown-up beside them.

What do I need?

Paper and a pencil or a pen, and crayons, markers or colored pencils for the colors. An eraser helps but isn’t needed.
