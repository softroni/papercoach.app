How to Draw an Orange Half

An easy step-by-step drawing for kids and beginners: 12 steps, about 4 minutes, on paper.

  • 12steps
  • 4 minabout
  • Colorat the end

Quick answer

Draw an orange half in 12 steps: draw the peel, finish the peel, start the inside circle, then carry on one line at a time and color it in. It takes about 4 minutes with a pencil or a pen.

  • Steps: 12
  • Time: about 4 minutes
  • You need: paper, a pencil or a pen, something to color with
  • From: the Fruits path in Paper Coach

A cut orange is mostly circles and straight lines. You draw two rings for the peel, a tiny circle in the middle, and eight spokes like a wheel. Then you give each piece a round top so it looks like real fruit segments.

  5. 4Finish the inside circle

    Then its right half. The thin ring between them is the peel.

  6. 5Draw the button

    Right in the center, draw a little circle the size of a button.

  7. 6Draw four spokes

    Four spokes go out from the button: up, down, left and right. Stop before the ring.

  8. 7Draw four more spokes

    Put one more spoke in each gap, slanting out. Now the wheel has eight.

  9. 8Round the right pieces

    Each piece needs a round top. Go down the right side, bumping from spoke to spoke.

  10. 9Round the left pieces

    Keep going up the left side until all eight pieces are closed.

  11. 10Color the pieces

    Color all eight pieces golden yellow.

  12. 11Color the peel

    Color the peel orange.

  13. 12Color the rest

    The space around the pieces is cream, and so is the button.

Tips

Draw an orange half well

Tips for drawing an orange half

  • Keep the gap between the two big circles thin, about the width of a pencil eraser. A thick gap makes the peel look like a tire.
  • Draw the first four spokes like a plus sign. Then each slanted spoke goes right in the middle of a gap, so all eight pieces come out the same size.
  • Stop every spoke a little before the inner ring. That leftover space is where the round tops of the pieces will bump.
  • When you round the pieces, make each bump touch the tip of one spoke and curve over to the tip of the next.

Common mistakes

  • The button in the center is drawn too big, so the spokes get very short. Keep the button small, about the size of a shirt button next to the whole orange. The spokes should be much longer than it.
  • Running the spokes all the way to the inner circle, leaving no room for the round tops. Lift your pencil a short step before the ring. A small gap there lets the cream show around each piece.
  • Coloring the space around the pieces yellow too, so the pieces blend together. Leave the thin spaces around the pieces and the button cream. That light color is what makes the eight pieces stand apart.

Make it your own

  • Turn it into a lemon half: keep the same lines, but color the peel and pieces in two shades of yellow.
  • Make a lime by coloring the peel dark green and the pieces light green, keeping the cream space around them.
  • Draw two orange halves side by side, one a bit bigger than the other, to look like a fruit just cut in two.
  • Try a pink grapefruit: color the pieces pink and the peel orange-yellow.

Questions

Good to know

How do I get all eight orange pieces the same size?

Place the up, down, left and right spokes first, like a plus sign. Then split each of the four gaps in half with one slanted spoke. Even gaps make even pieces.

Why does an orange half need two circles?

The outer circle is the edge of the fruit and the inner one is where the peel ends. The thin ring between them is the peel you color orange.

What is the little circle in the middle of the orange?

It is the center where all the pieces meet. Drawing it first gives every spoke the same starting point, so the wheel stays neat.

My circles came out wobbly. Is that okay?

Yes. Real oranges are not perfect circles. Just keep the inner circle about the same distance from the outer one all the way around.

How long does it take to draw an orange half?

About 4 minutes for the lines, and a few more to color it in.

Can kids follow this guide?

Yes, children from about six can follow it on their own, and younger ones with a grown-up beside them.

What do I need?

Paper and a pencil or a pen, and crayons, markers or colored pencils for the colors. An eraser helps but isn’t needed.
