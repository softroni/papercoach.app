How to Draw Mountains

An easy step-by-step drawing for kids and beginners: 10 steps, about 2 minutes, on paper.

  • 10steps
  • 2 minabout
  • Colorat the end

Quick answer

Draw mountains in 10 steps: draw the big mountain, draw the snow on top, draw the left mountain, then carry on one line at a time and color it in. It takes about 2 minutes with a pencil or a pen.

  • Steps: 10
  • Time: about 2 minutes
  • You need: paper, a pencil or a pen, something to color with
  • From: the Sky & Weather path in Paper Coach

Three peaks stand together here: one big mountain, with a smaller one peeking out on each side. Each peak is just a few straight lines with a zigzag of snow near its point. It is a good way to learn overlap, because the big mountain stays whole and the others seem to sit behind it.

Tips

Draw mountains well

Tips for drawing mountains

  • Place the big mountain near the middle of the page and leave open space on both sides, so the two smaller peaks have room.
  • Make each point lower than the last. The left point sits well below the big point, and the right point sits lower still.
  • Keep each zigzag in the top third of its mountain. Most of the shape should stay open for color.
  • Keep the ground lines at about the same level, so all three mountains seem to stand on the same land.
  • When you color, stop at the zigzag line and leave the tops empty. They get their cream color last.

Common mistakes

  • The lines of a smaller mountain cross over the big mountain's slope. Start and stop each new line right on the big slope. Nothing goes inside the big shape, so the smaller peak looks like it is behind.
  • The zigzag floats in the middle and never reaches the sides. Begin the zigzag on one slope and end it exactly on the other, so the snow is a closed shape you can color.
  • All three peaks end up the same height, so the scene looks flat. Keep the middle mountain clearly the tallest and the right one the smallest. Different heights make the group look natural.

Make it your own

  • Swap the colors around, so the big mountain is blue and the smallest one is green.
  • Add more points to each zigzag for a rougher, more jagged snow line.
  • Make the slopes steeper and the bases narrower for tall, sharp peaks.
  • Add a fourth small peak behind one of the side mountains, with its own little zigzag of snow.

Questions

Good to know

How do you make mountains look like they overlap?

Draw the front mountain first. Then start the next one on its slope and draw only the part you can see. Stopping at the slope makes it look like it sits behind.

How do you draw snow on top of a mountain?

Draw a zigzag across the mountain near its point, going from one slope to the other. Everything above that line is snow. A smaller mountain gets a smaller zigzag.

Which mountain should I draw first?

Start with the biggest one. The two smaller peaks grow out of its slopes, so its lines tell you where they begin and how tall they can be.

How big should the smaller mountains be?

Try making the left mountain about two thirds as tall as the big one, and the right mountain a little shorter than the left. Exact sizes do not matter, as long as the heights differ.

How long does it take to draw mountains?

About 2 minutes for the lines, and a few more to color it in.

Can kids follow this guide?

Yes, children from about six can follow it on their own, and younger ones with a grown-up beside them.

What do I need?

Paper and a pencil or a pen, and crayons, markers or colored pencils for the colors. An eraser helps but isn’t needed.
