How to Draw a Glider

An easy step-by-step drawing for kids and beginners: 9 steps, about 2 minutes, on paper.

  • 9steps
  • 2 minabout
  • Colorat the end

Quick answer

Draw a glider in 9 steps: draw the body, finish the body, draw the left wing, then carry on one line at a time and color it in. It takes about 2 minutes with a pencil or a pen.

  • Steps: 9
  • Time: about 2 minutes
  • You need: paper, a pencil or a pen, something to color with
  • From: the In the Air path in Paper Coach

A glider flies without an engine, so its shape does all the work. Seen from above, it's a slim teardrop body with very long, thin wings that stretch out straight on both sides. Draw this one to practice keeping long lines straight and making both sides match.

Tips

Draw a glider well

Tips for drawing a glider

  • Make the wings reach far: from tip to tip they spread a little wider than the body is long.
  • Keep each wing narrow, about as thick as the body is at its widest point.
  • Before you draw the right wing, look at where the left wing ends. Stop the right one the same distance from the body.
  • Put the tail wings close to the narrow end of the body. Each one is only about a quarter as long as a main wing.
  • Tuck the canopy just behind the round nose and keep it small, well inside the body's edges.

Common mistakes

  • The wings come out short and wide, so the glider looks like a regular airplane. Stretch each wing much farther than feels right and keep its two edges close together.
  • One wing is longer than the other, so the glider looks lopsided. Pick a spot for the right wingtip that matches the left one before you start the line.
  • The body ends in a round shape at both ends, like a sausage. Keep the nose round, then let both sides come together to a narrow point at the tail.

Make it your own

  • Choose your own colors, like a white body with red wings, keeping the canopy a different color so it stands out.
  • Draw a second glider much smaller and higher on the page so it looks farther away.
  • Draw three gliders flying in a row, each one a little smaller than the one before it.

Questions

Good to know

How do I keep the long wings straight?

Look at where the wing should end, not at your pencil tip, and move your whole arm in one smooth stroke. A slightly wobbly line is fine once colored.

Why does a glider need such long wings?

It has no engine, so long, thin wings help it ride the air and stay up longer. Drawing them long is what makes it read as a glider.

Where should the wings attach to the body?

Put them about a third of the way back from the nose, just behind the canopy. That leaves a long stretch of body for the tail wings at the end.

Is this glider drawn from the side or from above?

From above, like looking down on it in the sky. That's why you see both wings spread out and the canopy sitting on top of the body.

How long does it take to draw a glider?

About 2 minutes for the lines, and a few more to color it in.

Can kids follow this guide?

Yes, children from about six can follow it on their own, and younger ones with a grown-up beside them.

What do I need?

Paper and a pencil or a pen, and crayons, markers or colored pencils for the colors. An eraser helps but isn’t needed.
