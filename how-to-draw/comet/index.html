How to Draw a Comet

An easy step-by-step drawing for kids and beginners: 9 steps, about 3 minutes, on paper.

  • 9steps
  • 3 minabout
  • Colorat the end

Quick answer

Draw a comet in 9 steps: draw the ball, draw the upper edge, add the top licks, then carry on one line at a time and color it in. It takes about 3 minutes with a pencil or a pen.

  • Steps: 9
  • Time: about 3 minutes
  • You need: paper, a pencil or a pen, something to color with
  • From: the Space path in Paper Coach

This comet starts as a plain circle. Then a long tail made of pointed licks streams away from it, up to the left. A smaller flame sits inside the tail, and three colors finish it: yellow, orange and blue. It's a quick drawing that teaches you how a round shape and a flowing shape fit together.

Tips

Draw a comet well

Tips for drawing a comet

  • Keep the ball small, about the size of a coin. Leave most of the page open above it and to its left, so the tail has room to stretch.
  • Start the upper edge at the very top of the circle and the lower edge at the very bottom. That makes the tail as wide as the ball where it joins.
  • Have the two edges come closer together as they go, so they meet at one sharp tip far out at the upper left.
  • Point every lick the same way, toward the far tip. Make the licks near the ball a little bigger and the ones near the tip smaller.
  • Leave a band of space between the inner flame and the outer edge. That band turns yellow later, so it needs to be wide enough to color.

Common mistakes

  • The tail comes out of the side of the ball like a stick, and it looks thinner than the head. Join the edges at the top and bottom of the circle, so the tail wraps the back of the ball.
  • The licks point every which way, so the tail looks like a bush instead of something moving fast. Point every lick back toward the far tip, away from the ball. Then the comet looks like it is rushing forward.
  • The inner flame is drawn almost as big as the tail, and the yellow disappears. Draw the inner flame about half the tail's width and stop it well short of the far tip.

Make it your own

  • Flip it so the ball sits at the lower left and the tail streams up to the right.
  • Draw two or three comets of different sizes crossing the page, each with its tail pointing the same way.
  • Make the tail extra long and thin, reaching nearly across the page, for a comet that looks even faster.
  • Swap the colors: a green ball, an orange tail and a red inner flame.

Questions

Good to know

Which way should a comet's tail point?

In a drawing, behind the ball, so the comet looks like it is rushing forward. Here the ball leads at the lower right and the tail trails up to the left, with every lick pointing back along it. (A real comet's tail always points away from the Sun.)

How do I make the tail look like fire and not a spiky leaf?

Give the licks different lengths and let them curve a little, like flickers. Then add the smaller inner flame, so the tail gets a bright middle.

Should the ball overlap the tail?

Yes. Draw the circle first and whole, then start both tail edges from it. The ball stays in front, and the tail looks like it is streaming out behind it.

How can a child draw the licks more easily?

Draw them as little bumps that end in a point, one after another along the edge, like a row of small waves all leaning toward the tip.

How long does it take to draw a comet?

About 3 minutes for the lines, and a few more to color it in.

Can kids follow this guide?

Yes, children from about six can follow it on their own, and younger ones with a grown-up beside them.

What do I need?

Paper and a pencil or a pen, and crayons, markers or colored pencils for the colors. An eraser helps but isn’t needed.
