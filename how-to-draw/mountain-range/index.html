How to Draw a Mountain Range

An easy step-by-step drawing for kids and beginners: 12 steps, about 3 minutes, on paper.

  • 12steps
  • 3 minabout
  • Colorat the end

Quick answer

Draw a mountain range in 12 steps: draw the ground, draw the three peaks, draw the snow lines, then carry on one line at a time and color it in. It takes about 3 minutes with a pencil or a pen.

  • Steps: 12
  • Time: about 3 minutes
  • You need: paper, a pencil or a pen, something to color with
  • From: the Landscape path in Paper Coach

This mountain range starts with one level ground line and three peaks side by side. Two hills and four small pines go in front, so the mountains look farther away. It's a good lesson in overlap: whatever you draw in front hides part of what stands behind it.

Tips

Draw a mountain range well

Tips for drawing a mountain range

  • Draw the ground line about a finger's width above the bottom edge. That leaves room for the front hill to sweep up from the corner.
  • Make the middle peak a little taller than the outer two. The row looks like a real range instead of three copies.
  • Keep each snow line wobbly and close to the tip, about a third of the way down the peak.
  • Let the pines on the hills stay small, no taller than the hill they sit on. Small trees make the mountains feel big.
  • Color the blue mountain first and the dark green front hill last, so the darkest part ends up closest to you.

Common mistakes

  • Drawing the peaks floating above the ground line, with gaps at the bottom. Start and end every up-and-down stroke right on the ground line so the three peaks share one base.
  • Drawing the mountain lines through the hills. Stop the mountain lines where a hill crosses them. The hill sits in front, so it hides that part of the mountain.
  • Making the snow line a straight, flat cut across the peak. Draw it with small dips and bumps. A wobbly edge reads as melting snow, while a ruler-straight line looks like a hat.

Make it your own

  • Add snow to the middle peak too, with its own wobbly line, so all three peaks look like they share one winter.
  • Draw more pines along the front hill, keeping each one a little smaller as it gets closer to the mountain.
  • Try five peaks instead of three, with the tallest one in the middle and shorter ones at each end.
  • Color the mountain purple instead of blue for an evening look, and keep the gray snow caps the same.

Questions

Good to know

How do I make mountains look far away?

Put something in front of them. Here, the hills and pines overlap the bottom of the mountain. Anything partly hidden looks farther back, and small trees next to big peaks add to the sense of distance.

Why are the snow caps gray instead of white?

White snow on white paper would disappear. Gray gives the caps a clear color against the blue mountain, so you can see where the snow stops.

How do I draw a pine tree simply?

Draw a spiky top like a zigzag triangle, then a short trunk underneath. A very small pine can skip the trunk, which helps it look farther away.

Should the three peaks be the same size?

They don't have to be. Peaks that differ a little in height and width look more natural. Just keep all three bases on the same ground line.

How long does it take to draw a mountain range?

About 3 minutes for the lines, and a few more to color it in.

Can kids follow this guide?

Yes, children from about six can follow it on their own, and younger ones with a grown-up beside them.

What do I need?

Paper and a pencil or a pen, and crayons, markers or colored pencils for the colors. An eraser helps but isn’t needed.
