How to Draw a Brick Window

An easy step-by-step drawing for kids and beginners: 16 steps, about 5 minutes, on paper.

  • 16steps
  • 5 minabout
  • Colorat the end

Quick answer

Draw a brick window in 16 steps: draw the ledge, draw the frame, draw the arch, then carry on one line at a time and color it in. It takes about 5 minutes with a pencil or a pen.

  • Steps: 16
  • Time: about 5 minutes
  • You need: paper, a pencil or a pen, something to color with
  • From: the Around Town path in Paper Coach

You build this window from the bottom up. First comes a flat ledge, then a frame with a rounded top, then a band of brick wedges arching over it. You don't fill the wall with bricks. You place just six in small groups and let the eye fill in the rest, and two vines climb the sides to soften the straight edges.

Tips

Draw a brick window well

Tips for drawing a brick window

  • Make the ledge a little wider than the frame so it sticks out on both sides, like a real sill holding the window up.
  • Draw the middle wedge line first, then work outward to each side. That keeps all seven wedges close to the same size.
  • Line up the cross between the panes with the top center of the arch. If the gap leans, the whole window looks crooked.
  • Keep the brick groups small and far apart. Six bricks in three patches look more like a real wall than a full grid of bricks would.
  • Give the left stem gentle, wide waves. Tight wiggles make it look like a spring instead of a climbing vine.

Common mistakes

  • Drawing the arch band so thin that it blends into the frame line below it. Make the band about twice as thick as the frame. Step it out past the frame at each foot so the two shapes stay separate.
  • Lining up the loose bricks in a neat, even stack. Shift each brick a little left or right of the one above it so the group steps in and out like an old wall.
  • Putting the vine leaves in matching pairs straight across from each other. Switch sides as you go down the stem. Each leaf should sit a little lower than the one on the other side.

Make it your own

  • Color the arch and bricks brick red or orange instead of pink. Keep the ledge gray so it still stands apart from the wall.
  • Let the right vine grow as tall as the left one so leaves frame the window evenly on both sides.
  • Add one or two more brick patches lower on the wall. Keep each patch to two or three bricks.
  • Color the glass pale yellow instead of blue to make the window look lit from inside.

Questions

Good to know

How do I get the brick arch even on both sides?

Mark the top center first. Draw one side of the band, then copy its curve on the other side. Make sure both feet land the same distance out from the frame.

How do I make the wedges fan out the right way?

Picture a point below the window's center, like the hub of a wheel. Aim each short line at that point. The wedges will be wider at the outer edge of the band.

Why draw only six bricks instead of a whole wall?

A few bricks are enough to show that the wall is brick. Drawing every one would crowd the picture and pull the eye away from the arch and the vines.

What makes the vines look like they are climbing?

Draw each stem from the bottom up in one flowing line. Then add leaves on alternating sides. Bunching the leaves near the top makes the vine look like it is still growing.

How long does it take to draw a brick window?

About 5 minutes for the lines, and a few more to color it in.

Can kids follow this guide?

Yes, children from about six can follow it on their own, and younger ones with a grown-up beside them.

What do I need?

Paper and a pencil or a pen, and crayons, markers or colored pencils for the colors. An eraser helps but isn’t needed.
