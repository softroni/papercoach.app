How to Draw an Excavator

An easy step-by-step drawing for kids and beginners: 15 steps, about 4 minutes, on paper.

  • 15steps
  • 4 minabout
  • Colorat the end

Quick answer

Draw an excavator in 15 steps: draw the tracks, close the tracks, draw the track wheels, then carry on one line at a time and color it in. It takes about 4 minutes with a pencil or a pen.

  • Steps: 15
  • Time: about 4 minutes
  • You need: paper, a pencil or a pen, something to color with
  • From: the Wheels path in Paper Coach

An excavator starts as a long pill on the ground. A low box, a tall cab and a bent arm stack on top. The arm is the fun part. It climbs up to a round elbow, then bends back down to a bucket that hangs like a scoop. If you can draw a circle and some straight lines, you can draw this digger.

Tips

Draw an excavator well

Tips for drawing an excavator

  • Make the pill-shaped tracks about three times as long as they are tall. That leaves room for three wheels in a row.
  • Space the three wheels evenly, with a small gap between each wheel and the track line around it.
  • Keep the cab on the back half of the body so the dip in front stays clear for the arm.
  • Draw the two upper arm lines parallel, like a ruler leaning right. Then the elbow circle fits neatly across their tops.
  • Let the lower arm reach past the front of the tracks, so the bucket hangs out in open space.

Common mistakes

  • The arm stands straight up, so the excavator looks like it has a pole on top. Lean the upper arm up to the right, then slope the lower arm down to the right. The bend at the elbow is what makes it an excavator.
  • The body is drawn as tall as the cab, so everything looks top-heavy. Keep the body a low, flat box. The cab should rise well above it, about as tall as the body plus tracks.
  • The bucket is a round circle, and it looks like a ball on a stick. Curve the bucket's belly down to a point, then back up. That point is its digging edge.

Make it your own

  • Raise the bucket by making the lower arm shorter, so it looks like the excavator is lifting a scoop of dirt.
  • Draw a mound of dirt under the bucket with one bumpy line, as if it just finished digging.
  • Turn it into a long-reach digger by stretching both arm parts to twice their length.
  • Try other colors on the cab and arm, like green or red, and keep the tracks gray.

Questions

Good to know

How do I draw excavator tracks that look right?

Draw one long pill shape, flat on top and bottom and round at both ends. Then set three circles in a row inside it. The round ends and the wheels make it read as tracks.

How do I make the excavator arm bend?

Split the arm into two parts joined by a circle. The first part leans up to the right, the circle sits at its top, and the second part slopes down from there.

Where does the arm attach to the excavator?

It starts in the dip at the front of the body, just in front of the cab. Starting it there makes the arm look like it grows out of the machine.

What makes an excavator look different from a bulldozer?

The bent arm with a bucket at the end. A bulldozer pushes with a flat blade in front, while an excavator reaches out and scoops with its arm.

How long does it take to draw an excavator?

About 4 minutes for the lines, and a few more to color it in.

Can kids follow this guide?

Yes, children from about six can follow it on their own, and younger ones with a grown-up beside them.

What do I need?

Paper and a pencil or a pen, and crayons, markers or colored pencils for the colors. An eraser helps but isn’t needed.
