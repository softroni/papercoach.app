How to Draw a Pine Tree

An easy step-by-step drawing for kids and beginners: 8 steps, about 2 minutes, on paper.

  • 8steps
  • 2 minabout
  • Colorat the end

Quick answer

Draw a pine tree in 8 steps: draw the top layer, draw the middle layer, draw the bottom layer, then carry on one line at a time and color it in. It takes about 2 minutes with a pencil or a pen.

  • Steps: 8
  • Time: about 2 minutes
  • You need: paper, a pencil or a pen, something to color with
  • From: the Plants path in Paper Coach

This pine tree is built from three triangles stacked on top of each other, each one wider than the last, on a small box trunk. If you can draw a slanted line, you can draw this tree. Coloring each layer a different green makes it look full and layered, like a real tree.

Tips

Draw a pine tree well

Tips for drawing a pine tree

  • Keep the top triangle small, about the size of a thumbnail. Small at the top leaves room for the wider layers below.
  • Make each layer clearly wider than the one above it. The bottom triangle should stick out past the middle one on both sides.
  • Begin each new layer a little in from the corners of the flat line above, so the layers overlap and the tree looks connected.
  • Draw the trunk right under the center of the bottom layer. Keep it narrow, about as wide as the top triangle's base.
  • Color from light at the top to dark at the bottom. That change in green makes the layers easy to tell apart.

Common mistakes

  • All three triangles come out the same width, so the tree looks like a tall stack instead of a cone. Let each layer slant out farther than the last. The bottom layer should be the widest part of the whole tree.
  • The trunk is drawn too tall or too wide, and the tree looks like it is standing on a post. Keep the trunk short, about half the height of the top triangle, and narrow enough to sit well inside the bottom layer.
  • The layers float apart with gaps between them. Start each new layer right on the flat bottom line of the one above, so the slanted lines touch it.

Make it your own

  • Draw a small forest by making three or four pines side by side, some taller and some shorter.
  • Add a fourth layer at the bottom, even wider, for a tall grown-up pine.
  • Try coloring all three layers the same dark green for a darker tree, keeping the trunk brown.
  • Draw a tiny pine next to a big one, using the same three triangles at half the size.

Questions

Good to know

How do I make the triangles look even on both sides?

Put a light dot where the tip goes, right above the middle of the trunk. Slant both lines down from that dot, making them end at the same height.

Why does each layer start a little way in instead of at the corners?

Starting in from the corners makes the new layer tuck under the one above. The layers overlap like branches, so the tree looks like one piece.

How can I tell the three green layers apart?

Use light green on top, regular green in the middle and dark green on the bottom. The steady change from light to dark keeps each layer separate.

Is this a good first tree for a young child?

Yes. It uses only straight slanted lines and a small box. A child can draw one triangle while a parent draws the next, then trade.

How long does it take to draw a pine tree?

About 2 minutes for the lines, and a few more to color it in.

Can kids follow this guide?

Yes, children from about six can follow it on their own, and younger ones with a grown-up beside them.

What do I need?

Paper and a pencil or a pen, and crayons, markers or colored pencils for the colors. An eraser helps but isn’t needed.
