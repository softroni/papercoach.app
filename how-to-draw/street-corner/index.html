How to Draw a Street Corner

An easy step-by-step drawing for kids and beginners: 17 steps, about 5 minutes, on paper.

  • 17steps
  • 5 minabout
  • Colorat the end

Quick answer

Draw a street corner in 17 steps: draw the level lines, draw the corner, draw the walls, then carry on one line at a time and color it in. It takes about 5 minutes with a pencil or a pen.

  • Steps: 17
  • Time: about 5 minutes
  • You need: paper, a pencil or a pen, something to color with
  • From: the Around Town path in Paper Coach

This street corner shows a building from its edge, so you see two walls at once, each running away from you. Everything starts from one choice: where your eyes are. Put that height at the door, and the roofline, windows and ledges all tip toward it as they go back.

Tips

Draw a street corner well

Tips for drawing a street corner

  • Keep the two level lines at the same height on both sides. Every slant in the drawing points back toward that height.
  • Make the corner line the tallest line in the drawing. Both walls get shorter as they move away from it.
  • Tip each window's top the same way as the roofline above it. Bottom edges of the lower windows tip less because they sit closer to eye level.
  • Shrink the far windows on the right wall a little, both in height and width. That small change does most of the work of showing distance.
  • Draw the five awning bumps evenly spaced first, then run the four stripe lines up from where the bumps meet.

Common mistakes

  • Drawing the window tops perfectly level, so the walls look flat while the roof tilts. Lay a pencil along the roofline above each window and copy its tilt onto the window's top edge.
  • Putting the door's bottom far below the ground line of the wall. Stand the door right on the wall's foot line, which tips up as it runs back from the corner.
  • Making both walls the same length, so the corner looks like a flat sign folded in half. Let one wall run a bit longer than the other. Uneven walls feel more like a real building seen from a corner.

Make it your own

  • Swap the wall colors: an orange wall with the door and a yellow wall with the café.
  • Change the awning stripes to blue and cream, and match the flower boxes to them.
  • Give every upper window a flower box instead of a ledge for a corner full of green.
  • Try a different flower color in each bush, keeping them as small dots on the green.

Questions

Good to know

What is two-point view in a building drawing?

It means you see a building from its corner. Each wall runs away toward its own side, so its top and foot lines both slant toward eye level instead of staying flat.

Why do the walls tip down at the top and up at the bottom?

Your eyes are at door height. Lines above that height slope down as they go away, and lines below it slope up, so everything narrows toward eye level.

How do I make the windows lean the right way?

Match each window's top and bottom to the wall lines nearest them. Upper windows tilt like the roofline, while windows near the door are almost level.

How can I draw the awning so it looks striped?

Draw its scalloped bottom with five bumps, then add four lines between the stripes. Coloring alternates red and cream across the five stripes.

How long does it take to draw a street corner?

About 5 minutes for the lines, and a few more to color it in.

Can kids follow this guide?

Yes, children from about six can follow it on their own, and younger ones with a grown-up beside them.

What do I need?

Paper and a pencil or a pen, and crayons, markers or colored pencils for the colors. An eraser helps but isn’t needed.
