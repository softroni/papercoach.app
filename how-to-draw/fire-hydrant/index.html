How to Draw a Fire Hydrant

An easy step-by-step drawing for kids and beginners: 14 steps, about 4 minutes, on paper.

  • 14steps
  • 4 minabout
  • Colorat the end

Quick answer

Draw a fire hydrant in 14 steps: draw the collar, draw the dome, draw the barrel, then carry on one line at a time and color it in. It takes about 4 minutes with a pencil or a pen.

  • Steps: 14
  • Time: about 4 minutes
  • You need: paper, a pencil or a pen, something to color with
  • From: the Around Town path in Paper Coach

This hydrant is built from the top down. A flat collar comes first, and every other part hangs from it or sits on it. You'll add two nozzles, a stream of water curving into a puddle, and three paving stones, then finish with red, blue, gray and cream.

Tips

Draw a fire hydrant well

Tips for drawing a fire hydrant

  • Keep the collar fairly wide. The dome sits on it and the barrel hangs under it, so its width sets the size of the whole hydrant.
  • Make the two dome ribs follow the dome's curve, like lines on a ball. If they go straight down, the dome looks flat.
  • Place the side line a little right of the middle and let it run most of the barrel's height. That one line makes the barrel look round.
  • Draw the water stream thick and smooth, curving down from the knob in one stroke. Then make the puddle wider than the foot.

Common mistakes

  • The belt ends up too high or too low, so the barrel looks squashed. Put the belt where the barrel's sides stop. Leave a short, gently curved section below it before the foot flares out.
  • Both nozzles look the same, so the hydrant seems to face nowhere. Draw the right nozzle from the side, with its neck showing. Turn the left one toward you so you see its round cap face on.
  • The foot is the same width as the barrel and the hydrant looks like it could tip over. Flare the foot out wider than the barrel on both sides, so it looks firmly planted on the stones.

Make it your own

  • Leave out the water and puddle for a quiet street scene, and add more paving stones around the foot.
  • Color the hydrant yellow instead of red and keep the cream bands. Both colors are common on real streets.
  • Pour the water from the left nozzle instead, letting the curve fall toward you into the puddle.
  • Draw a row of three hydrants in different sizes standing on one line of paving stones.

Questions

Good to know

Why start with the collar instead of the dome?

The collar is a simple flat band, easy to place well. Once it's down, the dome sits on top and the barrel hangs below, so everything lines up with it.

How do I make the hydrant look round and not flat?

Curve the ribs over the dome, wrap the belt around the barrel with a slight curve, and add the long side line right of the middle.

How do I draw water that looks like it's flowing?

Use one thick, smooth curve that bends down from the knob into the puddle. Two small ripples on the puddle show where the water lands.

How big should the nozzles be next to the barrel?

Keep each nozzle short, about a third as wide as the barrel. The cap is a bit bigger than its neck, and the knob is smaller than the cap.

How long does it take to draw a fire hydrant?

About 4 minutes for the lines, and a few more to color it in.

Can kids follow this guide?

Yes, children from about six can follow it on their own, and younger ones with a grown-up beside them.

What do I need?

Paper and a pencil or a pen, and crayons, markers or colored pencils for the colors. An eraser helps but isn’t needed.
