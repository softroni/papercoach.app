How to Draw a Big Oak

An easy step-by-step drawing for kids and beginners: 9 steps, about 3 minutes, on paper.

  • 9steps
  • 3 minabout
  • Colorat the end

Quick answer

Draw a big oak in 9 steps: draw the left side, draw the right side, draw the left branch, then carry on one line at a time and color it in. It takes about 3 minutes with a pencil or a pen.

  • Steps: 9
  • Time: about 3 minutes
  • You need: paper, a pencil or a pen, something to color with
  • From: the Plants path in Paper Coach

A big oak is mostly about shapes that spread. A wide trunk splits into three branches, and a ring of big bumps wraps around them like a cloud. You build the trunk and branches first, then let the treetop curl around them.

Step by step

Draw it in 9 steps

  1. 1Draw the left side

    Begin at the ground on the left. Go up the trunk, then swing out and up to the left branch's tip.

  2. 2Draw the right side

    Now a flat line for the foot. From its end, go up the trunk and swing out to the right.

  3. 3Draw the left branch

    Round off the left branch's tip and come back down its inside to a sharp V.

  5. 4Draw the middle branch

    The middle branch is a tall thumb. From the V, go up, round the top, and down to a second V.

  6. 5Draw the right branch

    From that V, run up the inside of the right branch and round its tip to close it.

  7. 6Draw the treetop

    The treetop is a ring of big bumps. Start at the trunk on the left and bump your way up to the top.

  8. 7Finish the treetop

    Carry on down the right side, bump by bump, until you reach the trunk again.

  9. 8Color the treetop

    Color the treetop green, all around the branches.

  10. 9Color the trunk

    Color the trunk and branches brown.

Tips

Draw a big oak well

Tips for drawing a big oak

  • Make the trunk wide and short. It should look strong enough to hold all three branches.
  • Let the outer branches lean away from the middle one, so the three spread apart like fingers on an open hand.
  • Keep the two V shapes sharp and low. They show where the trunk ends and the branches begin.
  • Leave room above the branches before you start the bumps, so the treetop can wrap around them with space to spare.
  • Make the bumps big and a little different from each other. Five or six on each side looks fuller than many tiny ones.

Common mistakes

  • The middle branch ends up as tall as the treetop, so it pokes through the bumps. Stop the thumb shape well below the top of the page space, and start the bumps higher than its rounded end.
  • The bumps shrink as you go, so the treetop looks like a small scallop edge. Keep each bump about as wide as the trunk, from the left side all the way around to the right.
  • The treetop line ends away from the trunk and leaves a gap. Aim your last bump back toward the trunk early, so the line lands right on it and the shape closes.

Make it your own

  • Stretch the treetop wider than it is tall for a broad, sheltering oak.
  • Draw a shorter, fatter trunk and smaller bumps for a young, round little oak.
  • Use a lighter green near one side of the treetop to suggest sunlight falling on it.
  • Draw two oaks side by side, one bigger than the other, sharing the same ground line.

Questions

Good to know

How do I make a tree look like an oak?

Give it a thick trunk, wide branches that spread apart, and a broad, bumpy treetop. A wide, round shape reads as an oak more than a tall, narrow one does.

How do I draw the bumpy treetop evenly?

Start at the trunk on the left and draw one big curve at a time. Each bump starts where the last one ended, so the ring stays connected all the way around.

How many branches does an oak need?

Three is plenty for this drawing: one to the left, one to the right, and a tall one in the middle. They fill the treetop without crowding it.

Why do I color around the branches?

The trunk and branches sit in front of the treetop. Keeping the green outside their lines lets the brown shapes stay clear and easy to see.

How long does it take to draw a big oak?

About 3 minutes for the lines, and a few more to color it in.

Can kids follow this guide?

Yes, children from about six can follow it on their own, and younger ones with a grown-up beside them.

What do I need?

Paper and a pencil or a pen, and crayons, markers or colored pencils for the colors. An eraser helps but isn’t needed.
