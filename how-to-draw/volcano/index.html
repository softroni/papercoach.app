How to Draw a Volcano

An easy step-by-step drawing for kids and beginners: 11 steps, about 3 minutes, on paper.

  • 11steps
  • 3 minabout
  • Colorat the end

Quick answer

Draw a volcano in 11 steps: draw the crater, draw the left slope, draw the right slope, then carry on one line at a time and color it in. It takes about 3 minutes with a pencil or a pen.

  • Steps: 11
  • Time: about 3 minutes
  • You need: paper, a pencil or a pen, something to color with
  • From: the Sky & Weather path in Paper Coach

A volcano starts with a flat oval and two bumpy slopes, so it comes together fast. Then lava drips over the rim and smoke puffs up and drifts away. It's a good way to practice drawing one shape over another, and kids love the red-hot crater.

Tips

Draw a volcano well

Tips for drawing a volcano

  • Keep the crater oval flat and small, about a third as wide as the ground line. A narrow top makes the mountain look tall.
  • Let each slope spread out as it goes down, so the feet sit well past the ends of the oval.
  • Make the middle drip the longest, reaching about halfway down the mountain. The side drips stop higher up.
  • Overlap the smoke puffs a little, and make each one bigger than the last as they lean to the right.
  • Color the mountain and lava first, then the crater. Small parts are easier to fill once the big areas are done.

Common mistakes

  • The crater is drawn as a round circle, so the top looks like a ball sitting on the mountain. Squash the oval so it is about three times wider than it is tall. Then it looks like a hole seen from the side.
  • The slopes are perfectly straight, and the volcano looks like a party hat. Wobble the lines as you go down. Small bumps make it read as rock.
  • The lava drips end in sharp points, like icicles. Finish each drip with a round blob at the bottom, like a drop of thick syrup.

Make it your own

  • Add a fourth or fifth smoke puff, each one larger, so the cloud drifts across the top of the page.
  • Draw a second, smaller volcano behind the first, with its slope tucked behind the big one.
  • Make it sleepy: skip the lava and draw just one small puff of smoke over the crater.
  • Use two shades for the lava, orange near the rim and yellow at the round ends of the drips.

Questions

Good to know

How do I make a volcano look different from a regular mountain?

Give it a flat oval top instead of a peak. The open crater, the lava running over the rim and the smoke rising out of it all tell people it is a volcano.

Why do you draw the far edge of the crater first?

The far edge is the top curve of the oval. Drawing it first sets the width, and then the near edge closes the shape underneath without guessing.

How do I draw lava that looks like it is flowing?

Start each drip at the rim and pull it straight down, ending with a round end. Different lengths make it look like it is still moving.

Which way should the smoke go?

Let it drift to one side, like wind is pushing it. Here it leans right, with each puff a little bigger and farther over than the one below.

How long does it take to draw a volcano?

About 3 minutes for the lines, and a few more to color it in.

Can kids follow this guide?

Yes, children from about six can follow it on their own, and younger ones with a grown-up beside them.

What do I need?

Paper and a pencil or a pen, and crayons, markers or colored pencils for the colors. An eraser helps but isn’t needed.
