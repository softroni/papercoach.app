How to Draw a Snowflake

An easy step-by-step drawing for kids and beginners: 10 steps, about 3 minutes, on paper.

  • 10steps
  • 3 minabout
  • Colorat the end

Quick answer

Draw a snowflake in 10 steps: draw the center, draw the top arm, draw the upper right arm, then carry on one line at a time and color it in. It takes about 3 minutes with a pencil or a pen.

  • Steps: 10
  • Time: about 3 minutes
  • You need: paper, a pencil or a pen, something to color with
  • From: the Sky & Weather path in Paper Coach

A snowflake is one small circle with six matching arms around it, so once you can draw one arm, you can draw them all. Each arm goes up one side, out to a branch, over a pointed tip, and back down. Six little diamonds fill the gaps between the arms, and two shades of blue finish it.

Tips

Draw a snowflake well

Tips for drawing a snowflake

  • Keep the center circle small, about the size of a fingertip. The arms should be four or five times longer than the circle is wide.
  • Think of the arms in pairs. The top and bottom arms line up in one straight line, and each slanted arm lines up with the one across from it.
  • Turn your paper as you go so each new arm points up while you draw it. Then every arm feels like the first one.
  • Put each branch about halfway up the arm, so the arm has a stem below it and a pointed tip above it.
  • Draw the diamonds last, centered in each gap and close to the circle, so they don't touch the arms.

Common mistakes

  • The arms come out different lengths, so the snowflake looks lopsided. Make a light dot where the top arm's tip ends, then imagine a circle through it. Stop every other tip on that same circle.
  • The gaps between arms are uneven, with two arms crowded together. Place the top and bottom arms first in your mind. Each side arm sits halfway between them, so all six gaps match.
  • The branches only stick out on one side of an arm. After going out and back on one side, repeat the same branch at the same height on the other side as you come down.

Make it your own

  • Draw a whole flurry: one big snowflake in the middle and two or three smaller ones around it, each with six arms.
  • Swap the colors: make the arms blue-green and the diamonds light blue, or try pale purple for the diamonds.
  • Add a second pair of branches on each arm, a smaller one closer to the tip, for a fancier flake.
  • Leave the arms white and color only the center and diamonds, so the snowflake looks icy and bright.

Questions

Good to know

Why does a snowflake have six arms?

Real snow crystals grow in six directions as water freezes, so six arms looks right. In a drawing, six also makes three straight lines crossing at the center, which keeps it balanced.

How do I make all six arms the same?

Draw them in the same order every time: side, branch, tip, branch, side. Turning the paper so each arm points up helps your hand repeat the shape.

Can a young child draw this snowflake?

Yes. A child can draw the circle and straight arms with simple V tips, and a grown-up can add the branches. The diamonds and coloring are easy for any age.

What if my diamonds look like circles?

Give each one four corners: a point toward the center, a point toward the outside, and one on each side. Soften the corners a little, but keep them there.

How long does it take to draw a snowflake?

About 3 minutes for the lines, and a few more to color it in.

Can kids follow this guide?

Yes, children from about six can follow it on their own, and younger ones with a grown-up beside them.

What do I need?

Paper and a pencil or a pen, and crayons, markers or colored pencils for the colors. An eraser helps but isn’t needed.
