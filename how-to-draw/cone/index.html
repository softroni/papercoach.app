How to Draw a Cone

An easy step-by-step drawing for kids and beginners: 4 steps, about 1 minutes, on paper.

  • 4steps
  • 1 minabout
  • Colorat the end

Quick answer

Draw a cone in 4 steps: draw the base, draw the left side, draw the right side, then carry on one line at a time and color it in. It takes about 1 minutes with a pencil or a pen.

  • Steps: 4
  • Time: about 1 minutes
  • You need: paper, a pencil or a pen, something to color with
  • From: the Forms path in Paper Coach

A cone is one curved line and two straight ones. The base is the front half of a flat oval, and both sides lean in until they meet at a single point. Once you can draw this, you can draw party hats, traffic cones and ice cream cones.

Tips

Draw a cone well

Tips for drawing a cone

  • Keep the base curve wide and shallow. Its middle should dip only a little below its two ends, like a smile that is barely there.
  • Pick the tip first and put a small dot there. Aim both side lines at that dot so they meet cleanly.
  • Put the tip roughly above the middle of the base curve. Then the cone stands up straight.
  • Draw each side in one smooth stroke from the end of the curve. Slow, steady lines look straighter than many short dashes.
  • Color with strokes that run the same way, from the base up toward the tip. The purple will look even.

Common mistakes

  • The base is drawn as a straight flat line, so the cone looks like a flat triangle. Let the base sag into a gentle curve. That small dip in the middle is what makes it look round.
  • The side lines start partway along the curve instead of at its ends, leaving little hooks sticking out. Start each side exactly at the end of the base curve, where the curve stops.
  • The two sides miss each other at the top, leaving a gap or crossing into an X. Mark the tip with a dot before drawing the sides, and stop both lines right on it.

Make it your own

  • Make a tall, skinny cone by placing the tip high above a narrow base, or a short, wide one with a low tip.
  • Draw three cones in a row, each a different height, and color each one a different color.
  • Turn it into a party hat by coloring it in bright stripes that run up toward the tip.

Questions

Good to know

Why is the bottom of the cone curved instead of straight?

The bottom of a real cone is a circle. Seen from a little above, a circle looks like a flat oval, and the front of that oval is the sagging curve you draw.

Do I need to draw the back of the oval?

No. In this guide you draw only the front of the base, the part you would see. The two sides and the tip close the shape.

How tall should the cone be compared to its base?

A good starting point is a cone about as tall as its base is wide. After that, try taller or shorter ones and see how the shape changes.

How do I keep my cone from leaning to one side?

Put the tip straight above the middle of the base curve. If the tip drifts left or right, the cone tips over that way.

How long does it take to draw a cone?

About 1 minutes for the lines, and a few more to color it in.

Can kids follow this guide?

Yes, children from about six can follow it on their own, and younger ones with a grown-up beside them.

What do I need?

Paper and a pencil or a pen, and crayons, markers or colored pencils for the colors. An eraser helps but isn’t needed.
