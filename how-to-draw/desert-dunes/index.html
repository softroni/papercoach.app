How to Draw Desert Dunes

An easy step-by-step drawing for kids and beginners: 11 steps, about 3 minutes, on paper.

  • 11steps
  • 3 minabout
  • Colorat the end

Quick answer

Draw desert dunes in 11 steps: draw the sand, draw the tall dune, draw the round dune, then carry on one line at a time and color it in. It takes about 3 minutes with a pencil or a pen.

  • Steps: 11
  • Time: about 3 minutes
  • You need: paper, a pencil or a pen, something to color with
  • From: the Landscape path in Paper Coach

This desert shows depth without a single building. The front dune covers the bottoms of the two behind it. The far cactus is drawn much smaller than the near one. A crest line splits each dune into a sunny yellow side and an orange shadow strip, and that split makes flat humps look like piles of sand.

Tips

Draw desert dunes well

Tips for drawing desert dunes

  • Keep the sand line low, about a third of the way up the page. That leaves room above it for the tall dune to rise.
  • Make the tall dune on the left clearly higher than the round one on the right. Two humps the same height look flat and planned.
  • Start each crest line right at the top of its dune. Let it curve down to the edge, not straight across.
  • Draw the far cactus about a quarter the height of the big one. That size gap is what pushes it far away.
  • Color the sunny sides first. Then put the orange only in the strip past each crest line, so the yellow stays the biggest part.

Common mistakes

  • The front dune's line climbs as high as the dunes behind it and hides them. Keep the front dune low and slow, a gentle swell. Its top should stay well under the tops of the back dunes.
  • The two back dunes touch or merge into one lumpy shape. Leave a small stretch of flat sand showing between them, so each reads as its own hill.
  • The cactus arms come straight off the post like a stiff T. Bring each arm out sideways first, then bend it up. The two arms can bend at different heights.

Make it your own

  • Move the big cactus to the other side of the front dune. Keep the small one on a back dune so the near and far sizes still work.
  • Add a third, even tinier cactus on the farthest dune top to stretch the distance more.
  • Flip the shadows: color the strip on the other side of each crest orange, as if the sun moved across the sky.

Questions

Good to know

How do I make the dunes look like they overlap?

Draw the back dunes first, then the front dune across their bottoms. Wherever the front dune passes, stop the back lines. The hidden parts tell your eye which hill is closer.

Why does the far cactus sit on a back dune?

Things farther away sit higher on the page and look smaller. Putting the small cactus up on a back dune, and the big one low in front, makes the space feel deep.

What is a crest line on a dune?

It is the sharp ridge where wind piles the sand. One side faces the sun and the other side falls into shade, so the line marks where the color changes.

Which side of the dune gets the shadow color?

The narrow strip past each crest line gets the orange. The rest of the dune is the sunny yellow side. Keep the same side shaded on every dune so the light makes sense.

How long does it take to draw desert dunes?

About 3 minutes for the lines, and a few more to color it in.

Can kids follow this guide?

Yes, children from about six can follow it on their own, and younger ones with a grown-up beside them.

What do I need?

Paper and a pencil or a pen, and crayons, markers or colored pencils for the colors. An eraser helps but isn’t needed.
