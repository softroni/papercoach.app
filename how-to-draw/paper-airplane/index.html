How to Draw a Paper Airplane

An easy step-by-step drawing for kids and beginners: 10 steps, about 3 minutes, on paper.

  • 10steps
  • 3 minabout
  • Colorat the end

Quick answer

Draw a paper airplane in 10 steps: draw the top edge, draw the wing back, draw the top fold, then carry on one line at a time and color it in. It takes about 3 minutes with a pencil or a pen.

  • Steps: 10
  • Time: about 3 minutes
  • You need: paper, a pencil or a pen, something to color with
  • From: the In the Air path in Paper Coach

This paper airplane is a dart made of three thin triangles. All three start at one sharp nose point and fan out to the left. You only need straight lines, so it is a good first drawing for anyone who worries that they can't draw curves.

Tips

Draw a paper airplane well

Tips for drawing a paper airplane

  • Put the nose near the right side of the page. The plane stretches left from it, so it needs room on that side.
  • Start every long line at the same nose point. If the three folds meet in one sharp tip, the plane looks folded and crisp.
  • Make each fold a bit slimmer or lower than the one above it. The top wing is widest, the middle fold is a thin sliver, and the bottom fold hangs lowest.
  • Draw each line in one steady stroke. A ruler or the edge of a book helps younger artists keep the lines straight.
  • Keep the back edges short. They should be much shorter than the long lines running back from the nose.

Common mistakes

  • The long lines start at different spots, so the nose looks blunt or split. Mark a small dot for the nose first, then begin each long line exactly on that dot.
  • The middle fold comes out as wide as the top wing, and the plane looks flat. Aim the middle line just below the wing tip so the fold stays a narrow sliver.
  • The bottom fold doesn't touch the fold above it and leaves a gap. Bring the short closing line from its low point all the way up until it meets the middle fold's edge.

Make it your own

  • Turn the whole plane so the nose points up and to the right, as if it is climbing into the sky.
  • Swap the colors around, like a blue top wing and a cream bottom fold, to make your own paper.
  • Draw two or three planes in a row, each one smaller, so the far ones look farther away.
  • Add a dashed line behind the tail to show the path the plane flew.

Questions

Good to know

How do I make a paper airplane drawing look folded and not flat?

Give each of the three triangles its own color, and keep them different sizes. The color change at each fold line reads as paper bending.

Why does my paper airplane look lopsided?

Usually the wing tip is placed too high or too low. Keep the top line long and the back edge short. Then line the other folds up under that tip.

Can a young child draw this paper airplane?

Yes. It is only straight lines and three triangles. A parent can mark the nose dot and the wing tip, and the child connects them.

Which way should the paper airplane point?

In this guide the nose is on the right and the plane stretches left. You can flip it, but every fold still has to start at the nose.

How long does it take to draw a paper airplane?

About 3 minutes for the lines, and a few more to color it in.

Can kids follow this guide?

Yes, children from about six can follow it on their own, and younger ones with a grown-up beside them.

What do I need?

Paper and a pencil or a pen, and crayons, markers or colored pencils for the colors. An eraser helps but isn’t needed.
