How to Draw a Wave

An easy step-by-step drawing for kids and beginners: 10 steps, about 3 minutes, on paper.

  • 10steps
  • 3 minabout
  • Colorat the end

Quick answer

Draw a wave in 10 steps: draw the sea, draw the small wave, draw the back, then carry on one line at a time and color it in. It takes about 3 minutes with a pencil or a pen.

  • Steps: 10
  • Time: about 3 minutes
  • You need: paper, a pencil or a pen, something to color with
  • From: the Sky & Weather path in Paper Coach

This wave is just a few curves on top of a flat sea line. A small hump leads into one big sweep that hooks over at the top. Foam bumps, two inside lines and a few bubbles make it look like it is moving. Simple shapes come first, and the motion comes from how they curve.

Tips

Draw a wave well

Tips for drawing a wave

  • Keep the small hump low, about a quarter the height of the big wave, so the big one looks tall next to it.
  • Draw the back in one smooth stroke from the hump to the top. Stopping halfway leaves a bump that breaks the flow.
  • Make the hook curl forward past the hollow below it, so the crest looks like it is about to fall.
  • Bend the two flow lines the same way as the back of the wave, so the water seems to rush upward.
  • Place the bubbles just in front of the hook, where the spray would fly off.

Common mistakes

  • The crest ends in a point instead of a hook, so the wave looks like a hill or a shark fin. Keep curving past the top, forward and then down, until the line turns back toward the wave.
  • The hollow line misses the sea line and leaves a gap at the bottom. Scoop under the hook and keep going until the line touches the flat sea line.
  • The foam bumps are big and uneven, so they look like clouds sitting on the wave. Draw small, even bumps close together, running only from the back of the wave to the hook.

Make it your own

  • Draw two or three small humps behind the big wave instead of one, each a little lower than the next.
  • Make the hook curl tighter into a near circle for a stronger, more dramatic wave.
  • Add more bubbles in a loose arc off the crest, keeping each one a small circle.
  • Use a darker blue inside the hollow and a lighter blue on the back to show depth.

Questions

Good to know

How do I make the wave look like it is curling?

Let the top line keep going forward and bend down into a hook. Then scoop the hollow underneath it. The empty space under the hook is what makes it read as a curl.

Why does a wave need a small hump behind it?

The small hump gives the big wave something to rise out of. It shows the water building up, and it makes the big wave look taller by comparison.

What do the lines inside the wave do?

They show which way the water moves. Two curved lines that follow the back of the wave make it look like water rushing up toward the crest.

Can young kids draw this wave?

Yes. It uses one straight line, a few curves, small bumps and circles. An adult can draw the hook first if the curl feels tricky, and the child can add the rest.

How long does it take to draw a wave?

About 3 minutes for the lines, and a few more to color it in.

Can kids follow this guide?

Yes, children from about six can follow it on their own, and younger ones with a grown-up beside them.

What do I need?

Paper and a pencil or a pen, and crayons, markers or colored pencils for the colors. An eraser helps but isn’t needed.
