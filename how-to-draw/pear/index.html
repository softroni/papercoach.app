How to Draw a Pear

An easy step-by-step drawing for kids and beginners: 8 steps, about 2 minutes, on paper.

  • 8steps
  • 2 minabout
  • Colorat the end

Quick answer

Draw a pear in 8 steps: draw the left side, draw the right side, draw the stem, then carry on one line at a time and color it in. It takes about 2 minutes with a pencil or a pen.

  • Steps: 8
  • Time: about 2 minutes
  • You need: paper, a pencil or a pen, something to color with
  • From: the Fruits path in Paper Coach

A pear is a good shape for learning to draw something narrow on top and wide on the bottom. You draw it as two matching curved sides, then add a short stem and one leaf with a vein. It takes a few lines, and it is easy to try again.

Tips

Draw a pear well

Tips for drawing a pear

  • Keep the top of the pear about half as wide as the bottom. The narrow neck is what makes it look like a pear and not an apple.
  • Start the second side at the same top point as the first, so the two curves meet cleanly at the top and at the bottom.
  • Make the stem short, about as tall as the pear's neck is wide, and tilt it just slightly to the left.
  • Attach the leaf high on the stem, near its top, and let it point out to the right, away from the stem's lean.
  • Color with light, even strokes that follow the round bottom of the pear, then go a little darker for the leaf.

Common mistakes

  • The bulge starts too high, so the pear looks like a circle or an egg. Let each side run fairly straight down for the top third before it swells out wide and round.
  • The two sides don't match, so the pear leans or looks lopsided. Draw the right side as a mirror of the left, checking that the widest point sits at the same height on both.
  • The leaf is round at the end and looks like a blob. Bring both edges of the leaf together into one sharp point before curving back to the stem.

Make it your own

  • Draw a taller, skinnier pear by stretching the narrow top part longer before the sides bulge out.
  • Try a short, chubby pear with a very wide bottom and a small neck.
  • Flip the leaf to the left side of the stem and lean the stem to the right instead.
  • Draw two or three pears side by side in different sizes, each with its own stem.

Questions

Good to know

How do I make a pear look different from an apple?

Keep the top narrow and put all the roundness low. An apple is round all over, while a pear stays slim near the stem and only gets wide toward the bottom.

Why draw the pear in two halves?

Drawing one side at a time makes it easier to match the curves. You copy the first side turned around, so the pear comes out even.

Which way should the stem lean?

In this guide it leans a little to the left. A slight tilt looks more natural than a stem standing perfectly straight up.

Where does the leaf vein go?

Draw one straight line down the middle of the leaf, from where it meets the stem toward the sharp tip.

How long does it take to draw a pear?

About 2 minutes for the lines, and a few more to color it in.

Can kids follow this guide?

Yes, children from about six can follow it on their own, and younger ones with a grown-up beside them.

What do I need?

Paper and a pencil or a pen, and crayons, markers or colored pencils for the colors. An eraser helps but isn’t needed.
