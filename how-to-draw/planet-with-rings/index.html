How to Draw a Planet with Rings

An easy step-by-step drawing for kids and beginners: 7 steps, about 2 minutes, on paper.

  • 7steps
  • 2 minabout
  • Colorat the end

Quick answer

Draw a planet with rings in 7 steps: draw the planet's top, draw the planet's bottom, draw the outside edge, then carry on one line at a time and color it in. It takes about 2 minutes with a pencil or a pen.

  • Steps: 7
  • Time: about 2 minutes
  • You need: paper, a pencil or a pen, something to color with
  • From: the Space path in Paper Coach

A planet with rings is a ball with a flat band wrapped around it at a slant. The ring hides part of the planet, so you draw the planet in two halves, top and bottom, and leave room for the band. Then you add the ring's two edges, close the hole, and color the planet orange and the ring yellow.

Tips

Draw a planet with rings well

Tips for drawing a planet with rings

  • Stop the top curve where the ring will be on each side. That way the planet already looks like it sits inside the band.
  • Picture one whole ball before you start. The top curve and the bottom curve should line up as if they were parts of the same circle.
  • Make the outside oval about twice as wide as the planet, so the ring sticks out clearly on both the left and the right.
  • Keep the inside edge close to the outside edge all the way round. A thin, even band looks neater than one that gets fat on one side.
  • Color the planet first, then the ring. The strip of ring that crosses in front of the planet stays yellow, not orange.

Common mistakes

  • Drawing a full circle first and then the ring on top, so planet lines show through the band. Draw only the planet's top and bottom curves, and leave the strip where the ring crosses empty.
  • Making the ring a level oval that lies flat across the middle. Tilt the oval so it leans up to the right. The slant is what makes the ring look like it circles the planet.
  • Leaving the ends of the inside edge open beside the planet. Add one short line on each side where the inside edge meets the planet, so the hole in the ring is closed.

Make it your own

  • Color the planet blue or purple and the ring a pale gray for a cold, icy planet.
  • Tilt the ring the other way so the oval leans up to the left.
  • Draw a second thin oval just outside the first one to give your planet a double ring.
  • Dot small stars around the planet, or add a tiny circle nearby as its moon.

Questions

Good to know

How do I make the ring look like it goes behind the planet?

Let the planet cover the back of the ring. No ring line crosses the top of the planet. Only the front part of the ring, the inside edge, crosses over the planet's lower half.

Why is the planet drawn in two parts?

The ring covers a strip across the planet. Drawing the top and bottom separately keeps that strip clear, so the ring sits in front without extra lines to erase.

How much should the ring tilt?

A gentle slant works best. The right end of the oval sits a little higher than the left, about as high as the top of the planet's lower half.

Which real planet looks like this?

Saturn is the planet best known for its wide, bright rings. Jupiter, Uranus and Neptune have rings too, but they are thin and hard to see.

How long does it take to draw a planet with rings?

About 2 minutes for the lines, and a few more to color it in.

Can kids follow this guide?

Yes, children from about six can follow it on their own, and younger ones with a grown-up beside them.

What do I need?

Paper and a pencil or a pen, and crayons, markers or colored pencils for the colors. An eraser helps but isn’t needed.
