How to Draw Cherries

An easy step-by-step drawing for kids and beginners: 8 steps, about 2 minutes, on paper.

  • 8steps
  • 2 minabout
  • Colorat the end

Quick answer

Draw cherries in 8 steps: draw the front cherry, finish the front cherry, draw the cherry behind, then carry on one line at a time and color it in. It takes about 2 minutes with a pencil or a pen.

  • Steps: 8
  • Time: about 2 minutes
  • You need: paper, a pencil or a pen, something to color with
  • From: the Fruits path in Paper Coach

Cherries are a good way to practice two things at once: drawing a round circle and letting one shape hide behind another. You draw one full cherry, show just a sliver of the second, then join them with two stems that meet under a single leaf. It's a small drawing, so a beginner can finish it in one go.

Tips

Draw cherries well

Tips for drawing cherries

  • Start the front cherry's circle at the very top and come back to that same spot. If you start somewhere else, the ends are harder to join.
  • Make the cherry behind look about the same size as the front one. Only a crescent shows on the left, but it should curve like a full circle.
  • Let the stems bend a little as they rise. Make them about as tall as one cherry, so the fruit doesn't look tiny or lost.
  • Keep the leaf a bit smaller than a cherry. A long, narrow shape with a pointed tip reads as a leaf better than a round one.
  • Start the vein right where the stems meet and stop it just before the leaf's tip, following the leaf's curve.

Common mistakes

  • Drawing the back cherry as a full circle that crosses over the front cherry. Stop the back cherry's line where it touches the front circle. The front cherry covers the rest, and that overlap is what makes one look behind.
  • The two stems never quite meet, so the cherries look separate. Pick one meeting point before you draw, then run both stems up to it. The leaf grows from that same point.
  • Coloring outside the cherry outlines, so red spills onto the stems. Go slowly near the edges and leave the thin stems uncolored. Color the middle of each cherry first, then work out to the lines.

Make it your own

  • Flip the drawing: put the back cherry peeking out on the right and swing the leaf out to the left.
  • Spread the stems wider at the bottom so the two cherries hang farther apart, almost side by side.
  • Add a third cherry peeking out behind the other two, with its own stem rising to the same meeting point.
  • Use a darker red for the back cherry so it looks like it sits in a little shadow.

Questions

Good to know

How do I make the second cherry look like it's behind the first?

Draw the front cherry complete, then draw only the curve of the second that peeks out to the left. Stop where it meets the front circle. Hiding part of a shape is what tells the eye it sits behind.

My circle came out lumpy. Should I erase it?

No need. Real cherries aren't perfect circles, so a slightly uneven one still looks right. If you want a smoother circle next time, draw it in two halves, left side and then right.

Where should the leaf go?

Right at the point where the two stems meet. Swing it out to one side so it doesn't cover the stems, and keep it smaller than a cherry.

How can a young child draw cherries?

Let them draw two circles side by side first, without the overlap. Then add two lines up to one point and a leaf. The hiding trick can come on the second try.

How long does it take to draw cherries?

About 2 minutes for the lines, and a few more to color it in.

Can kids follow this guide?

Yes, children from about six can follow it on their own, and younger ones with a grown-up beside them.

What do I need?

Paper and a pencil or a pen, and crayons, markers or colored pencils for the colors. An eraser helps but isn’t needed.
