How to Draw a Knight's Sword

An easy step-by-step drawing for kids and beginners: 12 steps, about 3 minutes, on paper.

  • 12steps
  • 3 minabout
  • Colorat the end

Quick answer

Draw a knight's sword in 12 steps: draw the boss, draw the blade, draw the groove, then carry on one line at a time and color it in. It takes about 3 minutes with a pencil or a pen.

  • Steps: 12
  • Time: about 3 minutes
  • You need: paper, a pencil or a pen, something to color with
  • From: the Fantasy Objects path in Paper Coach

This sword starts from a round boss in the middle of the page and grows outward, so the long blade and the short grip always line up. You'll add a groove down the blade, a guard that curls like ram's horns, a striped grip and two bright jewels. Drawing it on a slant makes it feel like it's being held up, ready for a quest.

Tips

Draw a knight's sword well

Tips for drawing a knight's sword

  • Place the boss a little below the center of your paper. The blade is much longer than the grip, so it needs most of the room above.
  • Keep both blade edges on the same slant, an even width apart, until they meet at the point. Turning the paper can help you pull long, steady lines.
  • Make the groove start just past the boss and stop well before the tip. That gap at each end makes it read as a groove, not a crack.
  • Curl the two guard arms the same size and in mirror image, so the sword looks balanced on both sides of the boss.
  • Space the four wrap lines evenly along the grip and slant them all the same way. Five equal bands look neat and tightly wrapped.

Common mistakes

  • The blade edges drift apart or pinch in, so the blade looks bent or wavy. Draw both edges in one smooth stroke each, keeping the same gap the whole way, and only bring them together at the very tip.
  • The grip comes out as long as the blade, so the sword looks like a stick. Keep the grip short, about a third of the blade's length or less, and end it with a knob bigger than the grip is wide.
  • The jewels fill the whole boss and knob, so the gold disappears. Draw each jewel as a smaller circle inside, leaving a ring of gold showing all around it.

Make it your own

  • Swap the jewel colors, or make both the same, like two green jewels for a forest knight.
  • Change the grip bands to two colors you like, such as blue and white, still taking turns.
  • Tilt the sword the other way, with the point toward the top left, so it faces a different direction.
  • Make the guard arms curl tighter for a fancier sword, or open them wider for a bolder one.

Questions

Good to know

Why does the drawing start with the boss instead of the blade?

The boss is where every part meets. Starting there lets the blade go up, the grip go down and the guard curl out from one fixed spot, so nothing ends up off line.

What is the line down the middle of the blade?

It's a groove, sometimes called a fuller. On a real sword it makes the blade lighter. In the drawing it adds depth and shows which way the blade points.

How do I make the guard curl evenly on both sides?

Draw one arm, then draw the second as its mirror. Start both from the boss at the same height and match how far each one curls back in.

How do I keep the sword straight when it's drawn on a slant?

Picture one long line running from the tip through the boss to the knob. Keep the blade, groove and grip all following that same line.

How long does it take to draw a knight's sword?

About 3 minutes for the lines, and a few more to color it in.

Can kids follow this guide?

Yes, children from about six can follow it on their own, and younger ones with a grown-up beside them.

What do I need?

Paper and a pencil or a pen, and crayons, markers or colored pencils for the colors. An eraser helps but isn’t needed.
