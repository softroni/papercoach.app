How to Draw Rolling Hills

An easy step-by-step drawing for kids and beginners: 11 steps, about 3 minutes, on paper.

  • 11steps
  • 3 minabout
  • Colorat the end

Quick answer

Draw rolling hills in 11 steps: draw the ground, draw the back hill, draw the second hill, then carry on one line at a time and color it in. It takes about 3 minutes with a pencil or a pen.

  • Steps: 11
  • Time: about 3 minutes
  • You need: paper, a pencil or a pen, something to color with
  • From: the Landscape path in Paper Coach

This landscape is built from three arches sitting on one ground line. Each hill tucks behind the one in front of it, so the picture looks deep even though every shape is flat. Two pines and a sun finish it, and the colors get paler as the hills go back.

Tips

Draw rolling hills well

Tips for drawing rolling hills

  • Keep the ground line low, about a fifth of the way up the page, so the hills and sky have room above it.
  • Make the back hill the widest arch, but let the front hill run lowest, from one bottom corner to the other.
  • Stop each hill's line where it meets a hill in front. Overlap only works if the hidden part stays undrawn.
  • Make the right pine clearly smaller than the left one. The smaller tree reads as farther away.
  • Press lightly with the dark green on the front hill first, then compare it to the pale far hill before going darker.

Common mistakes

  • Drawing every hill as a full arch, so the lines cross through each other. Draw the front hill last and erase or skip any part of the back hills that it covers.
  • Closing the back hill's top, then setting the pine on top of the line. Leave a small gap in the arch and grow the trunk up from inside that gap.
  • Coloring all three hills the same green, which flattens the picture. Go dark green in front, middle green on the right, and pale green at the back.

Make it your own

  • Add a fourth, even paler hill peeking out behind the far one to push the distance back more.
  • Move the sun lower so it sits just above the far hill, like early morning.
  • Plant a third pine on the front hill and make it the biggest of the three.
  • Try a sunset palette: an orange sun and blue-green hills that fade toward gray at the back.

Questions

Good to know

How do I make the hills look like they are behind each other?

Overlap them. Draw the front hill's arch over the others and leave out the lines it hides. Then make the back hill the palest color.

Why is the far hill lighter than the front one?

Air makes faraway things look paler. Coloring the back hill pale green and the front hill dark green copies that, so the eye reads distance.

How do I draw a simple pine tree?

Start with a short, straight trunk. Above it, draw a tall pointed shape with zigzag edges, wider at the bottom and narrow at the tip.

Where should the sun go in a hill drawing?

High in the sky and away from the hilltops and trees, so nothing touches it. A clear space around it keeps the picture calm and easy to read.

How long does it take to draw rolling hills?

About 3 minutes for the lines, and a few more to color it in.

Can kids follow this guide?

Yes, children from about six can follow it on their own, and younger ones with a grown-up beside them.

What do I need?

Paper and a pencil or a pen, and crayons, markers or colored pencils for the colors. An eraser helps but isn’t needed.
