How to Draw a Dump Truck

An easy step-by-step drawing for kids and beginners: 14 steps, about 4 minutes, on paper.

  • 14steps
  • 4 minabout
  • Colorat the end

Quick answer

Draw a dump truck in 14 steps: draw the frame, close the frame, draw the cab, then carry on one line at a time and color it in. It takes about 4 minutes with a pencil or a pen.

  • Steps: 14
  • Time: about 4 minutes
  • You need: paper, a pencil or a pen, something to color with
  • From: the Wheels path in Paper Coach

This dump truck has its bed tipped up, as if it is pouring out a load. You draw it from the bottom up. The long frame goes first, then the cab sits on the front, and the slanted box bed rests on a single lift arm. Two round wheels finish it, and then you color each part.

Tips

Draw a dump truck well

Tips for drawing a dump truck

  • Make the frame the longest shape on the page. The cab covers only its front third, and the bed rides over the back.
  • Keep the two bumps on the frame wide enough for the wheels. Each tire should fill its arch with a little space left above it.
  • Tip the bed at a gentle slant, with the low corner near the cab and the high end over the back wheel.
  • Space the four ribs evenly along the bed. Mark where they go with light dots before you draw the lines.
  • Draw the hub about half as wide as the tire so you can still see a thick gray ring around it.

Common mistakes

  • The bed is drawn flat, so the truck looks like it is just carrying a box. Lift one end of the bed clearly higher than the other. The long top and long bottom should both slope the same way.
  • The lift arm pokes into the bed or floats in the air. Start the arm on the frame and stop it right at the bed's bottom edge, so it looks like it is pushing up.
  • The window is small and low, close to the frame. Put the window high in the cab, near the roof, and make it about half as wide as the cab.

Make it your own

  • Draw the bed flat on the frame instead of tipped, and leave out the lift arm, for a truck driving to the job.
  • Add a pile of small rocks or sand sliding off the high end of the bed.
  • Swap the colors: try a yellow bed with a blue cab, and keep the tires gray so they still read as wheels.
  • Make the bed longer and add more ribs for a bigger truck that hauls a heavy load.

Questions

Good to know

How do I make the bed look tipped and not crooked?

Keep the bed a neat box with its two long sides parallel. Only its angle changes. The low end sits near the cab, the high end rises over the back wheel.

Why does the frame have bumps on top?

The bumps make room for the wheels. Each tire tucks up into its arch, which makes the truck look heavy and planted on the ground.

How big should the cab be next to the bed?

Make the cab shorter in length than the bed but a bit taller than the frame is thick. It should stand up like a small square on the front end.

Can a young child draw this dump truck?

Yes. Every part is a line, a box or a circle. A parent can draw the slanted bed together with the child, then let the child add the ribs and wheels.

How long does it take to draw a dump truck?

About 4 minutes for the lines, and a few more to color it in.

Can kids follow this guide?

Yes, children from about six can follow it on their own, and younger ones with a grown-up beside them.

What do I need?

Paper and a pencil or a pen, and crayons, markers or colored pencils for the colors. An eraser helps but isn’t needed.
